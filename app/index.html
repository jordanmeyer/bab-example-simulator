YOUR ASSUMPTIONS

Set the season.

01 · Demand forecast

Normal demand, conditioned to be nonnegative, then rounded to whole units. The realized mean can differ from your input.

02 · Product economics

Landed unit cost is uniform across the range, independent of demand. One cost applies to the entire order in each scenario.

03 · Order choices & risk limit

An option passes only when the upper end of its 95% loss-probability interval is within your limit.

04 · Reproduce the experiment

10,000 shared demand/cost draws per run. Identical assumptions and seed reproduce the same outcomes.

MODEL NOTES

A precise simulation.
An assumed world.

Contribution = full-price sales + clearance recovery − the entire order’s landed cost − fixed launch cost. It is not company profit. A loss is strictly less than $0; break-even is not a loss.

The forecast is conditional. Negative normal demand is excluded, then remaining demand is rounded to integer units. Input mean and deviation describe the underlying normal, not the conditioned distribution. Costs are rounded to cents. Demand and cost are independent; real freight shocks or economic conditions could connect them.

The decision rule is explicit. Among the three options whose 95% Wilson interval upper bound meets the risk limit, choose the highest simulated mean contribution; ties favor the smaller order. No qualifying option means no recommendation. If both demand and cost are fixed, the result is exact under the model and no sampling interval is needed.

This is a synthetic class exercise. There is no demand estimate from real customers, capacity limit, service-quality effect or forecast validation. No information is uploaded or saved. More draws reduce sampling noise, not model error.
